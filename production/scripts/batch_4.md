## VOICEOVER SCRIPT (clean — feeds TTS directly)

Every label had been cut off his clothes. // Someone wanted him to stay anonymous forever.

The first of December, nineteen forty-eight. A man's body on a beach south of Adelaide.
No name.

The autopsy found no cause of death. No wounds. No marks. Nothing.

But his trousers had a pocket sewn into the lining that no tailor would have put there.
Inside it was a scrap of Persian poetry.

It said two words. It is finished.

He had a train ticket he never used, a bus ticket he did, and a half-smoked cigarette
pinned to his collar.

For seventy-four years nobody could name him. Then a historian matched DNA from hairs in
a face plaster cast made in nineteen forty-eight.

The full unredacted case file is below. It starts with the labels.

## HOOK MECHANICS (first 3 seconds)

**Spoken:** *"Every label had been cut off his clothes. Someone wanted him to stay
anonymous forever."*

- **Deliberate-act hook** — cutting labels is a choice, not an accident. Implies agency
  and a second party, which immediately doubles the cast.
- **Second sentence assigns intent** — converts a fact into a motive, so the viewer is
  now asking *who*.
- Object-first and gore-free: survives every ad-safe review tier.

## CTA MECHANICS (final 5 seconds)

- **"It starts with the labels"** — points at the single strangest artefact and implies
  the file opens on a clue nobody has followed.
- Restraint fits the noir register; the pack sells depth, not gore.
