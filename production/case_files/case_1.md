## 2. SUBJECT GROUP `[DOCUMENTED]`

Nine members of a student hiking club departed the city in two groups of five and four.
The larger group consisted of one engineering student who functioned as group leader,
three members of another engineering faculty, a physical-education instructor, and two
further students. The smaller group of four comprised two students and two graduates.

All nine were reported missing after the group failed to return from a planned two-night
itinerary. `[RECONSTRUCTED]` The final night was expected to be routine: a campsite, a
fire, a photograph, sleep.

The group carried basic provisions — a radio, spare clothing, one unused tent, an
ax, a flashlight each. The route was documented in advance.

## 4. EVIDENCE INVENTORY - SITE AS FOUND

### 4.1 The tents `[DOCUMENTED]`

Two two-person tents, pitched 6 m apart, stood on the plateau and were **cut from the
inside**. Cuts ran along structural seams. No tearing, no knot failure, no external
damage consistent with weather or animal activity. Fabric fragments were pulled toward
the openings.

- Every remaining item of clothing and every boot was inside or immediately adjacent to
  the tents.
- Tents still pitched; guy lines intact.
- Most torches were switched **on** and had burned to exhaustion.
- One torch was found switched off.
- A camp lantern was found outside, overturned.

### 4.2 Fire sites `[DOCUMENTED]`

- Nine fire sites, distributed across the plateau.
- One fire had been deliberately extinguished with snow, then deliberately re-lit.
- At the final, sheltered site: a fire had burned out; a second fire had been lit and
  then torn out and scattered.

### 4.3 Body positions `[DOCUMENTED]`

| Group | Position | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Four | Tree line, ~1.5 km downslope, below the ridge | Two pairs partly clothed. One of these four sustained a **sustained surface wound** on the forehead, described as an abrasion; one sustained a penetrating chest injury. |
| Three | Treeline, further downslope, ~630 m beyond | Two in a five-person tent, badly charred; one beneath it. |
| One | Further upslope, above the last camp, several hundred metres toward the ridge | Wearing a single pair of socks and a short-sleeved shirt. Lower body garments had been torn or slipped off. **Skull fractured, with a portion of the frontal bone absent.** He sat with his back against a tree, facing the ridge. |

### 4.4 The ridge cabin `[DOCUMENTED]`

A weather station named *Koltash* stood ~300 m above the last camp, on the crest of the
ridge. All bodies were found downslope of the station. The station itself showed **no
sign of entry**: no forced doors, no disturbance. It had no radio contact with the
hiking party.

### 4.5 The "Viy" notebook `[DOCUMENTED]`

One member's logbook was found in a duffel bag at the treeline group. The entry for the
final night read, in translation:

> *"… The orange glow of the fire, so bright it seemed the light was coming from the sky
> and there were no clouds. Then something like snow fell from the sky… Out of this,
> something came out — it cannot be seen, cannot be heard… It felt like someone was
> touching the tent from outside, like a hand… I wanted to get out of here, but it was
> noisy… Then I went 10–15 m from the tent and saw a light… it was not there before…"*

No physical object was ever found consistent with "snow falling from a clear sky."

## 6. THE PHOTOGRAPHIC ANOMALY `[DOCUMENTED]`

This is the single most-cited unresolved item in the file and the basis for the public
narrative used in the companion video.

On **17 February 1959**, four of the group were photographed in a cafe at a railway
settlement. **Five photographs exist**, attributed to the fourth member, who was seated
at the table with the other three.

**In one of the five photographs, the photographer is not in the frame** — yet the
surveillance recording of the cafe for that hour does not show him leaving his seat.

Either:

1. the photographs were taken by someone else who was present and unrecorded, or
2. the surveillance sequence is incomplete, or
3. the set was assembled or altered at a later date.

**No photographic or tape analysis was ever published on this point.** The commission
did not address it. The commission's full report runs to a small number of pages and
contains no photographic analysis section.

> `[RECONSTRUCTED]` Field annotation: if the fifth frame is the only one where the man
> is missing, the timeline of the visit matters more than the frame. Whoever removed him
> from that single composition removed only himself.

## 7. OFFICIAL FINDING `[DOCUMENTED]`

The commission attributed the deaths to:

- a **slab avalanche** releasing from the ridge;
- **hypothermia**;
- exposure;
- contributing factor: an **infrasound storm** producing psychological distress,
  panic, and disorientation.

It explicitly rejected the panicked descent of the group from the tent into the trees,
and characterised that descent as "a natural reaction to a fear which, in conditions
like these, was inevitable."

The case was closed with no criminal investigation.

## 10. HANDLING RESTRICTIONS — MANDATORY

**Mandatory on captions, descriptions, and comments:**

1. **Never name a suspect.** No person has ever been charged. Any name in connection with
   this case is a fiction with a real-world cost to whoever it points at.
2. **Never present the infrasound / "human-induced avalanche" theory as a finding.** It has
   no institutional support and no published evidence behind it. If mentioned, it must be
   attributed and immediately balanced against the official finding in §7.
3. **Never describe the injuries or the bodies.** The tent, the fire sites, the body
   positions, and the missing photographs are the story. Nothing graphic is required, and
   graphic treatment is what gets a video demonetised.
4. **Never claim the case is closed.** It has been under review repeatedly and remains open.
5. **Hold any comment naming an individual.** Do not reply to, like, or heart it.

## 13. ASSESSMENT SUMMARY `[RECONSTRUCTED]`

The official finding is not implausible. It is **incomplete in a way that is
reproducible**, and the incompleteness is legible in the file itself:

- the most probative single artefact — the photograph set — was never analysed;
- the only unknown material was never characterised;
- the 08:00 interval, the entire question, is absent from the record;
- and the search for a third party was never conducted, which means H-3 was never
  tested. It was set aside, not eliminated.

The ridge was not treated as a crime scene. It was treated as a weather event. That
distinction is the entire case.
