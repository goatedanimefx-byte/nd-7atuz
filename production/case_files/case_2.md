## 4. EVIDENCE INVENTORY

### 4.1 Site configuration `[DOCUMENTED]`

The farmstead comprised a dwelling, stable and barn, **interconnected**. Sound generated
in the barn was not audible inside the house — a physical fact that shaped the
investigation's reconstruction.

### 4.2 The weapon `[DOCUMENTED]`

- **Mattock** identified at autopsy as the implement used.
- A mattock bearing **dried blood** was discovered **hidden in the attic** during
  demolition — i.e. it was in the house at the time of the fire, and had been put away.
- A **penknife** was recovered from the hay in the barn.

### 4.3 Body disposition `[DOCUMENTED]`

| Location | Count | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Barn | 4 | V-1, V-2, V-3, V-4. Bodies stacked; covered with hay, sheets and/or clothing. |
| House — V-3's bedroom | 1 | V-5, aged 2, in his bed/bassinet. |
| House — maid's room | 1 | V-6, in her own bed. |

### 4.4 The surviving child `[DOCUMENTED]`

**This is the single most significant forensic finding in the case.**

V-4 (age 7) was found in the barn with **tufts of her own hair clenched in her fists**, and
further hair recovered from the straw. Forensic assessment was that **she remained alive
for several hours after the assault on herself**, and that this occurred while she was
lying in the straw among the bodies of her family.

> She was not protected. She was not hidden. She was in the middle of it, for hours.

### 4.5 The calendar `[DOCUMENTED]`

A wall calendar in the farmhouse was found open to **1 April 1922**. The page for
**31 March 1922** — the date the household was killed — had been **torn out**.

Inference in the historical record: the page was removed either by the perpetrator or by
someone else, after the event.

### 4.6 Fingerprints `[DOCUMENTED]`

Contemporary reporting records that police **failed to secure any fingerprints** from the
crime scene.

### 4.7 Sealed rooms `[DOCUMENTED]`

The maid's quarters and certain rooms were **boarded up from the outside** — a detail long
cited as inconsistent with an intruder narrative, and the source of persistent
"nobody could get in" claims. The boarding pattern was examined at the time.

### 4.8 The dog `[DOCUMENTED]`

The farm's dog — a Pomeranian, described as a small and persistent barker — **barked at
almost every visitor to the farm**.

The former maid **Kreszenz Rieger**, in interrogation, stated the dog **never barked at
one particular man** who had worked on the farm.

## 5. PERSONS OF RECORD — SUSPECTS ONLY `[DOCUMENTED]`

> **This section records names that appear in the historical inquiry. No individual listed
> here was arrested, charged, or convicted. Inclusion is not a finding of guilt.**

### 5.1 Anton Bichler

- Had assisted with the **potato harvest** at Hinterkaifeck and was therefore familiar
  with the premises and its layout.
- Rieger stated he had spoken often of the Gruber and Gabriel families, and reported him
  as having suggested at some point that **the family ought to be dead**.
- Rieger separately stated the dog **never barked at him**.
- Rieger also reported speaking with an unidentified stranger through her window late one
  night.

### 5.2 Schlittenbauer

- Remained the centre of local suspicion for decades.
- Comments attributed to him were treated as indicating knowledge of details only the
  perpetrator would recall.
- **Known to have disturbed the bodies at the scene**, which may have compromised the
  investigation. This is recorded as a fact about the evidence, not about his intent.

### 5.3 Other recurring theories in the record

- **V-3's late husband**, some of whom claimed had not in fact died during the First World
  War.
- **Revenge arising from the household's internal history** (see 2, autumn 1921).
- Political-era speculation, generally unsupported by the document record.

> `[RECONSTRUCTED - FIELD ASSESSMENT]` These are theories recorded because they were
> circulating in the historical inquiry. None is established. The document record supports
> **no conclusion at all** about identity.

## 6. INVESTIGATIVE FAILURE POINTS `[RECONSTRUCTED - ASSESSMENT]`

1. **No fingerprints were taken.** One hundred and four years on, no comparison is possible.
2. **The scene was demolished inside twelve months.** The demolition is documented as
   having *revealed* evidence (the attic mattock, the penknife), which implies the
   investigators did not search the attic and the barn hay before the site was destroyed.
3. **A suspect is recorded as having disturbed the bodies**, with the resulting
   contamination never formally quantified.
4. **Skulls were removed and shipped to Munich**, and the examination material does not
   appear in the accessible record.
5. **A page was removed from the calendar**, and the inquiry does not resolve by whom or
   when.

## 12. ASSESSMENT SUMMARY `[RECONSTRUCTED]`

The violence here was not improvised. A seven-year-old child survived for hours in the
middle of it. A page was torn from a calendar. The offender then stayed and ate.

Every one of those facts indicates **behaviour with intent**, not frenzy. Which means the
case was probably always solvable — and was made unsolvable by a scene demolished inside
a year, with no fingerprints taken, by investigators who had a living seven-year-old
survivor and a witness-quality dog.

The pack sells the evidence. It does not sell a solution, because there isn't one.
