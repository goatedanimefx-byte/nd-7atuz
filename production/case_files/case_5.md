## 4. THE VESSEL AS FOUND — CONDITION `[DOCUMENTED]`

### 4.1 Seaworthiness

- Vessel was **seaworthy and under a combination of sail**.
- **Not sinking. Not on fire. Not wrecked. Not under any apparent strain.**
- **c. 3 feet / 1 metre of water in the hold** — an amount that posed no threat and would
  not have caused alarm.

### 4.2 Cargo

- **1,701 barrels of denatured industrial alcohol — entirely intact.**
- Cargo had **not** been jettisoned.
- Cargo hatch covers had been **lifted off and left lying on deck**.

### 4.3 Provisions

- **Six months' worth of food and water remained aboard.**

### 4.4 What was missing

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **The lifeboat (yawl)** | Normally stowed over the main hatch. **Gone.** |
| **The ship's papers** | Removed. |
| **The captain's navigational instruments** | Removed. |

### 4.5 What was in order

- Cargo and personal belongings were **largely undisturbed**.
- The hull's long service as a coal carrier — **coal dust packed throughout** — is
  widely cited as a reason the amount of water in the hold could not have been measured
  from inside the vessel.

### 4.6 Loose items of note

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **The log slate** | Left aboard, bearing the final entry of 25 November. |
| **One pump** | Found **partially disassembled**. |
| **A sounding rod** | Lying nearby. |
| Sails and rigging | Damaged. |

> `[RECONSTRUCTED - FIELD ASSESSMENT]` The pump and the sounding rod are the two most
> interesting objects on the list, and they are routinely left out. Someone was measuring
> something in the hold. Someone who then dismantled the thing they used to measure it.

## 5. THE UNSOLVED CORE

Three questions, each documented, none answered:

1. **Why did the master order abandonment?** A two-year-old child does not go into a boat
   in an Atlantic gale by choice.
2. **Why was the vessel left seaworthy and provisioned?** A crew abandoning in panic does
   not lash a boat properly or tidy the hold.
3. **Where did ten people go?** No body. No wreckage of the yawl. No floating article.
   No survivor. No message. No carving. Nothing.

## 7. THE INQUIRY `[DOCUMENTED]`

The vessel was taken to Gibraltar and formally abandoned. A salvage claim was heard and a
British inquiry conducted.

**Finding: no evidence of foul play.**

The inquiry did not establish how or why the crew left, nor what became of them. It
established only that **crime could not be demonstrated.**

> `[RECONSTRUCTED - FIELD ASSESSMENT]` "No evidence of foul play" is not "no foul play."
> It is a statement about the limits of what could be proved from a floating box with no
> witnesses. The case is not unsolved because it was closed. It is unsolved because there
> was nothing left to examine.

## 13. ASSESSMENT SUMMARY `[RECONSTRUCTED]`

Ten people boarded a seaworthy, well-provisioned ship. Ten people were never seen again.
The ship itself was fine.

Every frightened explanation requires a mistake; every violent explanation requires
evidence that does not exist. What the file actually contains is a set of **removals**:
the boat gone, the papers gone, the instruments gone, and the ten people gone — from
a vessel left in order, with their belongings neatly behind them.

The hold had three feet of water and a dismantled pump and a rod for measuring it, on a
hull packed with coal dust that would have made the measurement meaningless. Someone was
trying to find out how much water was coming in. They found out. Then they all left in
the only boat, and the boat is never found again.

That sequence is not a solution. It is just the last thing that is known to have happened.
