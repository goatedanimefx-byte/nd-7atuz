## 4. THE *TAMAM SHUD* PAPER `[DOCUMENTED]`

### 4.1 The artefact

A small piece of rolled paper, found in a **fob pocket sewn into the lining** of the
deceased's trousers.

Bearing the words:

> **T A M A M   S H U D**

Written in the case file as *Taman Shud* in the inquest record; the correct transliteration
is ***Tamám Shud***.

### 4.2 Provenance of the text `[DOCUMENTED]`

The phrase appears as the closing line of the **final page** of a copy of the *Rubáiyát of
Omar Khayyám*.

- Detective Leonard Brown traced the phrase to the book.
- He identified the **edition** as sold by a specific Adelaide bookshop in Pulteney Street.
- The library, lending and university copies were checked — **none** had the page torn.
- The paper in the deceased's pocket was **determined to have been torn from a book**.

**The book from which the scrap was torn has never been identified.**

### 4.3 Translation `[DOCUMENTED]`

Persian. *Tamám* = "the end"; *shud* = past tense auxiliary, "became / was done".
**"It is finished." / "The end."**

### 4.4 Interpretations offered at the inquest `[DOCUMENTED]`

| Observer | Position |
|---|---|
| Prof. J. B. Cleland | Death almost certainly not natural; probably poison taken with suicidal intent. The words "support this considerably" and indicated the deceased was "fed up with things." |
| Detective Raymond Leane | "No fact I know of points towards suicide and abolishes the possibility of murder." Death unnatural, "but how I cannot say." |

### 4.5 Inscriptions in the book `[DOCUMENTED]`

The recovered copy contained, inside the back cover, a **handwritten note interpreted as a
possible code**, together with **an unlisted telephone number**. The number was traced to
a woman. Both she and the man associated with the number were interviewed.

**Neither appears anywhere in the inquest file.**

> `[RECONSTRUCTED - FIELD ASSESSMENT]` This is the largest documentary hole in the case.
> An interview that produced evidence was conducted, and the record of it is missing.
> Nobody can say what was said, or why the material was kept out of the file.

## 6. THE SECOND MAN `[DOCUMENTED - LINKAGE CONTESTED]`

On 8 December 1948 — **eight days** after the beach discovery — a man was found collapsed
in a street in Adelaide and died. Items recovered from him were connected by police to the
Somerton Man's effects.

**This connection is one of the most repeated claims about the case and it is not
solidly sourced.** The specific popular detail — that the second man was found holding the
**other half of the same chocolate tablet** the Somerton Man had in his pocket — does not
appear in the State Records account of the case, which instead emphasises the *Taman Shud*
paper, the excised labels, the unexamined suitcase, and the inscriptions and telephone
number inside the book.

> **RULE FOR THIS CHANNEL:** do not narrate the half-tablet as established fact. If used
> at all, it must be framed as "a claim that has circulated for decades and cannot be
> verified." A `[DOCUMENTED]` tag on that scene would be a **false** tag.

## 13. ASSESSMENT SUMMARY `[RECONSTRUCTED]`

Every label was cut off. That is the fact the case is really about — not the paper, not
the poisoning, not the spy stories.

A man who did not want to be found would not have travelled by public bus to a beach and
died in the open. A man who wanted to be found would not have had every label removed.

Both cannot be true. He may have intended one thing and met another, or someone else
prepared him for the end. **The file does not know, and it has had seventy-four years
and one hair to find out.**

The hair was found. The name is not confirmed.
